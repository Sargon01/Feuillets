# Feuillets — writing first

> **English** · [Français](PHILOSOPHIE.md) · [Index](README.md)

> The author's work is to write. The software's work is not to get in the way.

An instrument born from writing, not from a theory of writing.

---

## Principles

1. **Text before system.** Write before configuring.
2. **Plan without becoming trapped.** A plan is a hypothesis, not a contract.
3. **Structure serves writing**, never the reverse.
4. **Information when it becomes useful**, not constantly in view.
5. **Measure what helps**, not everything that can be measured.
6. **Rewrite without losing work.** Versions should make it possible to take risks.
7. **No imposed writing method.** Gardener, architect, or both.
8. **A text can become a project** even if it was not created as one.

> Start writing. Feuillets will be there when your texts need to become a project.

The aim is the simplicity of a notebook, with a complete workshop that appears only when the author needs it.

---

## Obsidian provides the environment; Feuillets provides the workshop

Feuillets is not meant to become a standalone application hidden inside Obsidian.

Obsidian provides the vault, Markdown, links and backlinks, the editor, Canvas, the synchronization chosen by the user, and its plugin ecosystem.

Feuillets adds what is specific to long-form writing: a manuscript representation, structural operations, Research, working Context, narrative views, a rewriting workflow, coherent composition, and editorial tools.

This separation matters. Feuillets should not rebuild what Obsidian already does well.

## A tool born from writing, not from a theory

Feuillets did not begin with the aim of reproducing another writing application in Obsidian or with a list of features collected from author software. It began with a more concrete experience: writing books.

Writing tools often ask too much of the author. Some are powerful but complex; others are elegant but limiting as a manuscript grows. Many encourage the author to model a book before writing it: sheets, places, arcs, chronologies, beats, subplots, databases, tables and properties.

In Obsidian, the opposite problem can appear: almost anything is possible, but only after assembling plugins, templates, properties, queries and conventions. The author ends up designing the system before being able to work in the text.

Feuillets should reduce that friction, not add another layer.

## The notebook and the pen

The most accurate image of Feuillets is probably not a cockpit, dashboard or narrative database.

**It is the notebook and the pen.**

A notebook does not ask for the genre, character list, narrative structure or finishing date before accepting a sentence. You open it and write.

A sentence. A list. An image. A line of dialogue. A scene that may never find its place. A beginning. A memory. A research note. A paragraph tried and abandoned.

Feuillets should preserve this fundamental freedom. The author should never have to prepare the software before beginning the text.

## The project can come after the writing

A Feuillets project need not be the starting point. **It can be the consequence of a text beginning to take shape.**

Someone may begin with a folder of ideas, possible beginnings, scenes and dialogue, alongside research notes, articles, photographs and sources. For days or months it can remain exactly that: no mandatory Binder, project form or required metadata.

Then the fragments become an article, story, essay, collection or novel. Only then should a simple action mean: **use this folder as a Feuillets project**.

The author should not have to start over, migrate work into a proprietary database or rename files to obtain writing tools. YAML mapping and linked Research folders extend the same principle: **Feuillets adapts to the vault as far as possible, not the reverse.**

## Write before structuring — without opposing writing and planning

This philosophy does not mean planning is useless. Authors plan, some extensively and some lightly. They prepare lists, possible chapters, a chronology, essential steps, cards or a detailed outline.

The problem begins when software turns the plan into a contract.

> **A plan is a hypothesis about the book to come. The real book appears through writing it.**

A scene reveals what the plan did not anticipate. A character becomes more important. A plot becomes unnecessary. Two chapters must merge. A prepared ending is no longer the right one.

That is normal. Feuillets should make serious planning possible while keeping it inexpensive to change one's mind.

## Writing means rewriting

A serious manuscript is not produced in one pass. Writing also means trying, cutting, moving, splitting, merging, rewriting, abandoning, restoring, comparing and returning to an earlier direction.

Snapshots, comparison, backups, versions and annotations have distinct roles. They should protect the work without turning rewriting into repository administration.

**Continuous** answers the same need from another angle: revise several sheets as one long passage while keeping each source file separate.

## Text is the centre of gravity

The manuscript is not the accidental product of a database.

**Text is the centre of the project.**

Research, chronology, labels, goals and views exist to help write and revise that text. They are not more real than the sheet itself.

Feuillets therefore keeps Obsidian's editor at the centre. **Continuous** remains an editor of the real source files; **Preview** remains a reading and composition surface, not a second source of truth.

## No mandatory encyclopedia

A fiction project may contain a rich sheet for every character, place, object and historical event. It may also contain almost none.

Research should be available when the book needs it, not as homework before chapter one. The same applies to metadata: status, labels, dates, threads, goals and properties can enrich a project but remain optional. An empty Markdown file remains a perfectly valid starting point.

## Structural tools should remain tools

Binder, Cards, Outline, Story arcs, Timeline and Notebook are useful because they show **the same manuscript from several angles**.

> **One structure, several representations.**

Outline inspects structure and information. Binder navigates and moves. Split view shows the hierarchy at a glance. Continuous lets the author work across several sheets. None should create a second truth for the manuscript.

### Information should appear when it becomes useful

The **Context** section of the **Sheet** tab illustrates this principle: it surfaces Research or chronological information when the current passage makes it relevant.

The author should not have to keep the entire project bible open at all times.

A good piece of software should know how to be quiet.

## From gardener to architect — and back

The gardener/architect opposition is useful only if it remains mobile. An author may discover ten scenes freely, then build a precise outline, then abandon part of it because the text reveals a better direction.

Notebook helps the gardener capture and rearrange possibilities. Binder helps the architect give them a working order. Outline helps inspect structure. The author can move between them as often as necessary.

No mode should lock a project into a writing doctrine.

## From short text to book

A good writing environment should not require the author to know the final size of the text before beginning. A sheet can remain an article, note, short story or essay fragment. Several sheets can become a series, collection, chapter or book.

File, folder, selection and project scopes let the form of publication emerge after writing begins.

## Notebook: capture before organizing

Notebook is where structure can remain provisional. Ideas can be captured as cards, grouped, connected, split, merged and developed as idea trees before real manuscript files are created.

When a structure becomes convincing, the author can materialize it gradually in Binder. The relationship is not a permanent synchronization.

**Notebook helps thinking. Binder remains the real structure of the manuscript.**

## From fragment to book, then from book to editor

The work does not stop when the manuscript becomes readable. The author must also compose, export, send, receive corrections, decide which changes to accept and produce a new editorial version.

DOCX Review exists because the manuscript must remain Markdown even when editorial exchange happens in Word. Native collaborative review answers another case: exchanging a version with a Feuillets reviewer without exposing the whole vault or preventing the author from continuing to write.

## What authors' needs show

Authors generally need less software management, not more. They need to start quickly, find the right passage, understand structure, move scenes safely, keep useful documentation nearby, compare versions, spot continuity problems, assemble the real book, export it and exchange revisions without abandoning the source manuscript.

A feature is useful when it reduces friction in one of these actions.

## Progressive power

Feuillets may contain many tools without presenting all of them at once. The essential path can remain:

> Binder → sheet → Focus → Preview

As the project grows, Research, Cards or Outline, Continuous, Timeline, Journal, Review, DOCX Review and advanced Edition settings can become useful.

Power is not permanent interface density.

## What Feuillets must not become

Feuillets must not become a mandatory narrative database, a form to fill before writing, a general-purpose PKM, a hidden standalone application, a race to reproduce every commercial writing tool, or a system where metadata matters more than text.

Before adding something, ask whether it helps write, reread, find, reorganize, rewrite, finish or share the text. If the only justification is producing more data, visualization or control, its usefulness should be questioned.

> **The number of possibilities in Feuillets must never become the number of things an author has to understand.**

## Maintenance: stability before feature speed

Feuillets already provides a broad writing workshop. Maintenance should therefore prioritize stability, consistency between views, ergonomics and small improvements that genuinely help authors.

A rearchitecture or new feature is not an improvement by itself. It is worthwhile only when it makes writing, rewriting or sharing a manuscript simpler.

## An author-centred design

The most important difference is not that Feuillets has more features than another tool. That competition would never end.

> **Feuillets is designed from the author's work, not from a computer model of writing.**

The software does not try to explain how to build a story, turn a manuscript into data or decide whether a chapter works. It provides a place where the author can write, change direction, move, delete, begin again, return to an earlier state and gradually discover the shape of the text.
